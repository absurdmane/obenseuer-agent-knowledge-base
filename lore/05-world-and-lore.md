## 1. The fungal disease

### 1.1 Names and reach

The disease is called "fungal disease" in almost every text: the dialogues, the notes, the tasks and the newspaper. The abbreviation SMV appears in some dialogue lines, a sleep event and the ending. Only one source gives the full name, Stalburg Mushroom Virus, in a dialogue with the mushroom boutique owner Jasper Sporefield [274:25-26], who describes it as a fungal disease going around, a strain that makes fungi hostile to people, and says most people think a lab engineered it. Nobody knows where it came from. The item texts never name SMV, and the localisation notes say the same. The full name is therefore weakly supported (see 06-open-questions.md).

The disease is linked to green glowing mushrooms in most texts. Its reach is district-wide. The Stalburg Times in 2017 reported 72 new cases in a single weekend, and expected growth. Scientists disagree on how it spreads. The official line in the sources is that the government has done nothing for years (per Fredrik), and that the disease is serious enough to justify a quarantine zone.

### 1.2 Symptoms and course

The sources describe the disease as follows:

- **Pain, weakness and mental change.** Infected people have pain, weakness, confused or aggressive behaviour, and symptoms that a doctor can check. The unused doctor [31] checks for mental health problems and SMV, and sets a lifetime countdown in days (SMVLifetimeLeft).
- **Growths.** Fungus grows inside the body. Visible "wort growths" can be removed surgically for a fee (125 real money in the doctor's text).
- **Glow.** Infected people's eyes or skin glow green. Pia's eyes glow green. Jasper's text calls a glowing infection a sign; the item "Glowing Shit" is described as looking like someone got infected.
- **Heads and hands.** Infected people can have their heads destroyed by fungus; some are changed into "vegetables", and others are immune and still function (Colton counts himself among the lucky ones, [127:34-36]).
- **Death and body.** The dead and infected are collected by the Fungal Disease Treatment Unit. The unit uses five-metre distance rules, decontaminates buildings, and disposes of hosts. In the ending a subject's body is full of mushrooms yet still alive, and the fungus is red.
- **Red fungus.** The red fungus appears in a victim [84:11], on corpses near the gate [77:132], in the quarantine zone [77:153], and in the red extract given to Pia. Red mushroom extract is a bribe item for Casper [435].
- **Sleep risk.** A sleep event can give the player an infection while they rest in the open [168].
- **Hallucination.** Mushrooms cause hallucinations; the sources do not tie the infection itself to voices.

### 1.3 Treatments

Treatment in the sources is weak or experimental:

- **Medicine from Fredrik.** It keeps Pia alive and is a drug nobody fully understands. Fredrik cannot explain why it works for her alone (Pia's account).
- **Pharmacy fungicide.** Over-the-counter and prescription fungicide exists. The pharmacist Mona Hall says it is not very effective against the fungal disease, but nothing better is available [89:42].
- **A facemask** protects against fungi (item text).
- **Experimental drugs.** Fredrik gives the patient in his operating room red mushroom extract, and the result is aggression and a body that cannot take it. In another case, a letter says an experimental drug, given to a sick man without asking, left him cured or doing better. The ending shows that the drug did not stop the spread.
- **Mushroom substitute pills.** Synthetic compounds that help against mushroom addiction, given on prescription.
- **Priest's medicine.** Priests and shops sell medicine.
- **The fungal treatment unit** cleans buildings, including Hotel Korso, and cleaned a rat outbreak.

### 1.4 Quarantine and the government

- **Diagnosis and quarantine.** People diagnosed with the disease are sent to a quarantine zone, and some are thrown across the canal (per Finlay, who saw the Quarantined gang). Some patients escape the zone and hide among other people with masks.
- **The quarantine zone in Obenseuer** was announced in 2017 (Stalburg Times). Another zone was planned for Whiprock island. The mayor Jean Wallin asked people to avoid areas with many cases, especially Obenseuer and Uhlu.
- **Relocations.** The city moved whole buildings out (Deekula B9, within one day, with passports checked and mushroom products banned).
- **Gaps in the rules.** Relocated residents may carry what they can handle. Quarantine is closed and heavily watched. Some quarantined people are sent out on rafts or smuggled out in vans.
- **Travel.** Ken Nakahara was held at an airport screening and not allowed to leave. Officials blocked travel out of Obenseuer. Some passports are issued only occasionally.

### 1.5 Origin

Mr. Duck says the disease began on Turnip hill. Mushroom farmers found green mushrooms in an abandoned building, different from others [93:29]. The intake interviewer warns the player to stay away from green mushrooms [83:24], and the Bazaar's agreement with the government demands strict controls on green mushrooms. Jasper calls the origin unknown. The sources do not agree on how far the disease has spread, or whether it began as a lab accident.

## 2. Mushrooms and the drug culture

### 2.1 The main drug: green mushrooms

Green mushrooms are the main drug and the main source of the disease. They come in four strengths in the item data: low, medium and high bags, and a pure form that is "without ill effects" (psychoactive but not harmful). Unpure green mushrooms are a separate, lower grade. Green mushroom extract is a very potent form. The mushrooms are sold openly in the Bazaar (the co-op's downstairs vendor) and by phone in Deekula.

### 2.2 Other mushrooms

- **Blue glowing mushrooms** ([COPYRIGHT]). Ivar describes a blue mushroom cave where he felt dizzy and woke outside. He calls the cave cursed.
- **Orange glowing mushrooms** are ground into orange mushroom powder and pure orange mushroom powder. Orange mushrooms have bad side effects: hunger, thirst, sleepiness and loss of bowel control. Malone says orange shrooms never became popular for that reason. Henry Kauha died after using orange mushrooms heavily (in Emil's account). Ivar grows orange mushrooms and sells powder that makes miners faster and sharper.
- **Red mushrooms** are a rumour until the news reports a body covered in them. They are the basis of the extract used in Fredrik's experiment and the "red danger" of the quest lines.
- **Plain mushrooms** ("without effects") are sold too, as bags of mushrooms.
- **Winstead cigarettes** are mushroom-laced. A **mushroom rollie** is a hand-rolled one.
- **Mushroom coffee, kilju, beer, sahti and water** exist as glowing variants (the "glowing" prefix).
- **Shroom Water** is a mushroom drink. Osmo Olut is described as a mushroom drink in one source (Mikkel calls it rusty nails soaked in mushroom water).

### 2.3 Mushroom culture

- **Glowing** is the mushroom-laced prefix. Nearly every food, drink and drug has a glowing variant: glowing bread, sausage, pizza, sandwiches, soup, hot dogs, fish, sewer fish eggs, mash, drinks, and even urine.
- **Mushroom addiction** is an acknowledged problem with a treatment. Substitute pills are prescribed for it.
- **Betel palm nut** is an addictive nut, sold separately.
- **Mushroom rollies** are a currency of favour (Pera asks for one).
- **Mushroom lanterns** are made in the lantern factory in Tenement A. A lantern made of a head is the Crazy Neighbor's.
- **The vending machines**: Ivar's machine sells shrooms and orange powder. A "shroom automat" is mentioned in a henchman's note.
- **Mushroom visions**: mushrooms show "the other side". Mike says they let people see it, [COPYRIGHT]. The sources frame these as hallucinations or as true visions; the texts do not say which.
- **Mushroom tourism**: the Bazaar is a tourist destination for "shroom tourists", and the Harri group calls Obenseuer the capital of mushrooms.

### 2.4 Mushroom law

- The Kolhola rules ban possessing, consuming or growing green mushrooms and any products made from them, and evict violators at once.
- The relocation note bans all products of mushroom origin in one relocated building.
- The Bazaar and the government agreed to strict controls on green mushroom use (per Karl Hagen's arrangement with the authorities).
- Some mushrooms are legal and certified (Jasper's mycelium), with special permissions.

## 3. The red mushroom and the red rocks

- **The red mushroom** is named in the news (a body in a field covered in red mushrooms), in the notes (a body near the gate with red mushrooms growing on it), and in the experiment. The extract kills or harms the patient in the operating room, and Pia dies after being given it.
- **The red rocks** are a field of red stones. The phone voice tells the player to collect them. Welhö warns not to touch them. Touching one makes it vanish without visible change (conv 250). The Osrat notes say the red stones may cause the pull towards the other side. A black pendant (item) has a red stone that feels warm.
- **The red extract** is a bribe item (Casper asks for it) and a lure in the experiment.

## 4. Radiation, the repository and the accidents

### 4.1 The repository

The mine holds radioactive waste that should not be there. The old mine's repository is behind a gate (code [COPYRIGHT]), with a dumping tunnel where spent fuel casks are stored. One cask fell and broke open, making the tunnel deadly radioactive. Minutes inside give a fatal dose, and you feel nothing. A blast door is wired to radiation meters and does not open when radiation is above 50 millisieverts per hour. Radiation is a slow, silent killer. A dosimeter in sieverts is needed. A doctor should be seen after radiation exposure.

The waste is blamed on corruption and cost-cutting. Heska guesses that it came from the Black Rock power plant, and that enriched uranium might have been diverted for a bomb (his guess; the sources say nothing firm).

### 4.2 Radioactive items

Polonium-210 (a container of radioactive material), Cobalt-60 ("highly radioactive"), a broken sample container with a warning to seek medical attention, and a dosimeter are all items. They sit in a sample-and-science thread that the texts do not explain.

### 4.3 The metro accident (1986)

A metro train ended up in an S.N.W bunker after a signal error in 1986. Mason Jokinen says the surviving passengers were executed to keep the programme secret, and that the programme ended afterwards. He says he is not sure of the details. The S.N.W programme moved nuclear materials under the city for defence.

### 4.4 The Stalburg Nuclear Power Plant accident (2016, as one NPC tells it)

Anatoli Mustaruoho worked at the Stalburg Nuclear Power Plant (SNPP), on a small lake downstream of the Two Gorges Dam. In his account, on 9 August 2016, after riots and an election day and during power cuts, a dam failure upstream caused a flood. The plant was short-staffed. The boss sent most staff home and called an engineering company for a paper review. The reactor did not shut down automatically, the backup generators failed, a secondary generator in the containment basement was used, and a worker entered the reactor hall. Radioactive steam was vented. Anatoli was blamed and fired. No other source confirms the account.

### 4.5 Hammer Valley and the 2014 legend

A tunnel accident in Hammer Valley is recalled by Robin (the note in B3 storage) and by Ken's wish to see the place. The legend article of 2014 describes an underground city under the station, with a mushroom cult.

## 5. The other side and the devices

### 5.1 What the texts say

The other side is a realm that can be reached through a gate or through mushroom visions. It is described in three ways:

- Welhö's expedition went there; in his telling the place damages the mind, everyone on the expedition lost their sanity, research stations were left behind, and the Welho mark refers to a gateway.
- Mike says mushrooms show the other side, and that it is probably where everyone ends up. He says he hopes he is wrong.
- Shadows and ghosts are on the other side, or between the two sides. Father Tobias says the dead are in between, and that the player is from the in-between too.

The texts never define the other side. They hint at a supernatural layer and at a state of being in between.

### 5.2 The Osrat device

Osrat is a device that detects and connects to signals from the other side. It runs on AA batteries. It needs an amulet to get a signal (a black amulet found in the priest's flat). The device opened the big gate for the player, and when it did so, something spoke about G.A.L.E.N. A note says the best signal is near dead bodies, but not always. A strong signal makes it beep, after which the user can travel to the other side. The writer has only reached a state called black noise and needs a stronger transmitter. The maker of the device is someone Anya promised not to name.

### 5.3 G.A.L.E.N

G.A.L.E.N is a system voice at the city gate. It warns an entity not to enter the enclosed area, and not to shut down or interrupt G.A.L.E.N, which could be lethal to an unknown number of entities. A connection (7905) will be disconnected. The sources do not say what G.A.L.E.N is. The voice also speaks about it through the Osrat device.

### 5.4 The black world

Krista Annina describes the black world as a place under the ground where trapped souls are kept. Her ritual uses a green mushroom dream and a charm to protect the player. The black world may be a hallucination; the texts do not say.

### 5.5 Ghosts and the wasteland

The ghosts are the shadows of people who died: a murdered woman in a shed, a father looking for his daughter (Jacob), a friend mourned by another ghost, a wife who wants her husband to stop drinking, a man whose flat burned while he slept, and Mike's shadow, which is still tied to his body and the tunnel key. Their dialogues describe chains, darkness and voices. The wasteland lies outside the city, and some ghosts ask the player whether they are dead.

### 5.6 The phone voice

A voice on a phone line asks whether the mission is done and says no one has survived it. It tells the player to collect the red rocks. Its message is that the player was chosen for hope, desperation and stupidity.

## 6. Economy and currencies

### 6.1 OC and RM: what the sources support

The texts use three money words: "Open Sewer Coin", "Real Money" and plain "money" or "RM". The item texts say Open Sewer Coin is "a local coin" and Real Money is "a legal tender". The sources never write the abbreviations OC or RM. Reading the item texts with the dialogue, the most likely mapping is:

- **OC** = Open Sewer Coin, the local currency of Obenseuer, used for daily purchases (kebabs, cigarettes, rat-bet stakes, drinks), some wages (65 OC per hour at the lantern factory, 45 OC per hour for telemarketing, 140 OC per hour at the casino, 110 OC per hour for a dishwasher) and janitor services (435 and 620 OC).
- **RM** = Real Money, the legal tender, used for passports (100 RM), rent, fines, bridge tolls (2000 RM lifetime pass), co-op fees (1300 RM), greenhouse equipment, bribes and larger payments.

This is an inference from the item texts and from how prices are spread across dialogue. The sources do not confirm it. One Bazaar dialogue set glosses RM as Stollandian Marks; that reading does not contradict the mapping above. Another source says the meaning of OC is unclear. Treat the mapping as inferred, and see 06-open-questions.md.

### 6.2 Prices and wages (as the sources give them)

- Passport: 100 RM (from David Lund; Hank's route about 100 RM; a registration fee at the gate 100 RM).
- Bridge lifetime pass: 2000 RM (refused above 1000 RM for the whole bridge).
- Miner's tax: 200 RM.
- Co-op course: 100 RM; co-op membership fee: 1300 RM.
- Greenhouse equipment: Hydroponic Farm 13000 RM, Hydroponic Farm With Supports 10000 RM, Vertical Farm 2500 RM, Vertical Farm Box 60 RM.
- Fredrik's experiment reward: 1500 RM.
- Telemarketing: 45 OC per hour; OS magazine subscription 64 OC per week.
- Firewood fill for a tenement: 1400 OC.
- Osmo Minitrain: 2500 OC.
- Pig: 400 OC, or 1000 OC at full price.
- Döner: 200 RM cash (Irma Kött).
- Machining: 22 RM per hour. Sewing: 10 RM plus 100 OC per hour. Casino security: 140 OC per hour.
- Renovation: installments; the first installment, then the final payment on completion.
- Janitor: 435 OC (water tanks) and 620 OC (septic tanks).
- Decontamination: about 600 RM, one to four days.
- Puuhari: 24 RM per month; ARG: 12 RM per month.
- Open Homes luxury house: 220 000 RM.
- Greenhouse: see above.
- Bribes: 100 RM to 10000 RM for the speed camera man; 150 RM for the industrial zone; 1000 RM for the quarantine van; 100 RM for a casino security guard; 10 RM for a sauna bribe.

### 6.3 Money, work and debt

- **Work** is found by walk-in for manual jobs (machining, sewing, mining, the lantern factory). Office jobs need an application.
- **Mining** needs a permit and a tax. Ore is sold to the ore buyer for a receipt. Claim owners pay workers 20 percent of the receipt value.
- **Debt.** Samuel Godman's invoices and unpaid debts show a formal debt system. Bank accounts can be frozen and unfrozen for a fee.
- **Bank.** Stalburg Bank accounts have debit cards that can be lost or confiscated, and netbank for loans. Balance checks by phone are refused.
- **Bottles.** Empty glass bottles can be returned at the O-Market for a few coins.
- **Stolen goods.** Stolen goods are sold to traders (Yuri Takahashi trades with the player if they hold stolen goods worth over 1000).
- **Counterfeits.** Fake money is "worthless counterfeit money". It is an active problem.
- **Scam.** A scam invoice lures people to pay into a bank account.
- **Gambling.** Ace of Spades tickets at kiosks, a casino, rat bets, card games and a heavy gambler (Heikki).
- **Subsidies.** The Sossu subsidy exists (its details are unclear). A state disability pension can be refused.

### 6.4 Housing and rent

- The player owns or renovates tenements, and can rent apartments to tenants. Rent income and rent payments are menu settings, so the economy can be tuned.
- Demand is high: many people sleep on the street, live in pod hostels (Warehom Hostel), or wait years in the Sossu queue.
- Tenants often cannot pay. Some are evicted (Malone's branch, the Crazy Neighbor's branch).
- Renovation takes days and is done in stages. Some contractors work for installments.
- Deeds, blueprints and energy certificates are rare. [COPYRIGHT] lacks an energy certificate (Open Homes).
- The Chamber of Commerce needs upgrades before it can open a shop.

### 6.5 Scrap and crafting

Scrap metal recycles at 5 to 1; plastic at 10 to 1; brass, silver and gold at 1 to 1. Gold can be extracted at ratios 10 to 1, 2 to 1 and 1 to 1. A gold bar is low-purity gold alloy; a 1 kg high-purity gold bar exists. These are crafting values, not lore. Furniture blueprints (crude, improved, detailed) and the LMAO-Tables book of formulas are crafting unlocks.

## 7. Law, crime and police

- **Trespass** into someone else's home or a restricted area leads to prison or a teleport, depending on who notices.
- **Theft** is common. Lock picks are sold. A dog plush alerts the owner to thieves. Dishwasher powder is used to launder stolen goods.
- **Police** are present but weak. They take reports, arrest for crimes, and sometimes bribe. Bribes are paid in OC; fines in RM. A failed bribe puts the player in a cell.
- **Prison** holds people in cells with lockers. A judge sets the sentence. The guard gives food on a schedule. The jail's lobby has a warden.
- **Sentence and fines.** Prison time has a multiplier (0.7, 1 or 1.25). Fines are paid in RM.
- **Emergency line 112.** Calls go to the police and, for infected people and bodies, to the fungal treatment unit.
- **Fraud.** Scam invoices and fake passports.
- **Vigilantes and gangs.** Svamppatrullen and Sköldgangster fight; the Quarantined is a violent young gang.
- **Murder and missing people.** Pia's and Alice's deaths, the B3 bathroom body, and Ines's killings. The police attend slowly.
- **Bodies.** The FDTU collects the dead and infected. Smuggling people in vans is possible.

## 8. Technology and devices

- **Telephones** are the main channel for many services: the bank, Osmo Olut's customer service, the Swamp Publishing magazine, Hobbyprof, the toll bridge, the telemarketers, Fungispring, the FDTU, the Open Homes Company and Legal Remedies. Some numbers cannot be called from this network [103].
- **Netbank** is the bank's online service. It is required for loans.
- **Elevators** are installed by Urho Siltanen and Jonne Reis. The elevators in Obenseuer are said to be in better condition than elsewhere in Stalburg, but the Tenement A elevator is out of service.
- **Doorphones and keypads** control most buildings. Keycards open the pharmacy and Tenement A.
- **Surveillance cameras** record people. The bank cameras can be shut off from the boss's room.
- **Minitrains and speed cameras** run the mine's track. Speeding is caught by cameras and fined.
- **The Osrat device** (above).
- **Drones** deliver goods into the quarantine. They are disabled by the enemy.
- **Flood doors and floodgates** in the Bergmann tunnels, with a red and green light system.
- **Radiation meters and dosimeters**. The blast door in the dumping tunnel is linked to radiation meters.
- **Retro electronics** are a niche market (Jonas Elström).
- **Printers** in the Management office.
- **Passports** are paper documents; forged ones are made by hand (Eliana, David Lund).
- **The gate** is a keypad that reads four digits aloud.
- **The interview room** has big screens, and the interview uses a number that is also the gate code.

## 9. Daily life

- **Food.** Potatoes, turnips, cabbage, leek, peas, carrots and sugar beet are staples. Plenty of tinned food. Sawdust is used as a flour substitute in bread and pizza. Pea soup from the Redemption church. Turnip soup from Julius's mother. Street food in disposable bowls and cups.
- **Drink.** Home-made wine (turnip, potato, carrot, mushroom), moonshine in the same variants, kilju, sahti (strong and tasty; very strong for the vakisahti), beer in pints, cider, applejack, rice wine, kumis, shochu and milk moonshine. Drink culture is home-made and often mushroom-spiked.
- **Meat.** Chicken, pork, beef, sewer fish, rat, cat and dog. Mystery meat is sold as-is. Dog meat is described as a meat from your best friend. Cat meat is a dark joke in the bread and pizza variants.
- **Sewer food.** Sewer fish, sewer fish eggs and glowing sewer fish.
- **Sleep.** Beds have comfort levels from torturous to comfy. [COPYRIGHT] is a message.
- **Work.** Pay is low, the work is hard, the workplaces are noisy.
- **Hygiene.** Showers, sinks and toilets are expensive or broken. Some have "no urination" rules.
- **Pets.** Cats are sold (Emma-Maija Taylor). Pets are a source of jokes. Anna's cat Kisu is a plot point.
- **Leisure.** Card games (Crosseven, Shittypants), cinema, sauna, rat betting, guitar playing, the internet cafe, Maahis Mättö (a game) and TV news.
- **Routine.** The jail has a routine; the Bazaar has opening hours; the Kolhola rules set quiet hours.
- **Health.** Doctor's visits need a turn ticket (Dr. Pena). The pharmacy needs a prescription.

## 12. Politics and institutions

- **Stolland** is the national government and the country. The item texts also use the name for vodka and the sources use it for the government.
- **Stalburg** is the wider city. The city's population includes the Bazaar, the Central district and the Obenseuer block.
- **Obenseuer** is the tenement district and the mining district. Its name is also used for the canal, a metro, a pharmacy, a magazine and the sewer scene. Whether it is a district or the city is unclear.
- **Open Sewer** is the locals' name for Obenseuer, after the canal (per Omari). It also appears in several company and product names (OS Mining Services, OS Power, the Open Sewer magazine). The official status is unclear.
- **The government**: refuses to expand Obenseuer; stopped issuing new passports; blocked entry and exit to the quarantine. The government trained Elias Karhu to spot lies. It is criticised for handling the disease badly and for doing nothing for years.
- **The Bazaar**: a semi-independent enclave with its own gate, passports, jail, bank and Chamber.
- **The mines' management** sets the rules (the founders' rules).
- **The Chamber of Commerce** wants growth, expulsion of the slums, and to absorb the player's tenement.
- **Redemption**: a church and aid group in the Bazaar.
- **Police** and the **FDTU**: the authorities on the ground.
- **Stalburg University of Technology** and student housing association.
- **The land registry** handles notarised deeds.

## 13. History as the sources give it

### 13.1 The city's history (from the notes and the Stalburg Times)
- The ore mine was dug by hand by Henry Kauha. The mine closed in 1958, according to one note; another says half the district was still mining until the 1970s and was then rezoned for housing (see 06-open-questions.md).
- The municipality of Obenseuer merged into the City of Stalburg in 1973.
- The 1970s: the area was zoned for housing. Walter Co. built the tenements. Buildings were left unfinished after inspection failures for ground stability and contaminated soil; locals moved in anyway.
- 1981: Stalburg Steel collapsed.
- 1986: an S.N.W metro accident (above).
- 2014: the Stalburg Times covered Obenseuer's decline (21 September) and the Underground City legend (9 November). The legend: homeless people live in an underground city with waterfalls and colours (happy version), drug users and a cult (dark version), or a psychoactive mushroom farm with captives (wild version). No proof.
- 2016: the SNPP accident (one NPC's account; see section 4.4).
- 2017: Stalburg Times (13 February) reports the government will open a quarantine zone in Obenseuer. Mayor Jean Wallin asks people to avoid Obenseuer and Uhlu. 72 new cases in one weekend.
- Later: the Deekula B9 relocation.

### 13.2 The Bazaar's history
- The Bazaar's leaders quarrelled with the government over passports, expansion and delivery problems.
- The Bazaar and the government agreed to controls on green mushrooms in exchange for tourists.
- The Bazaar's bank was robbed by Heikki Mailanen with the manager's help.

### 13.3 The mines' history
- Henry Kauha built the old mine by hand; he died after using orange mushrooms heavily.
- The Devil's Angels grew orange, then green, mushrooms under Malone.
- Robin, the former Bergmann tunnel worker, broke the flood door, bricked up a storage and died.
- A Bazaar team broke into the western tunnels and found the radioactive deposit.

### 13.4 Politics of the present
- The Bazaar and the government are at odds.
- The Chamber fears central government.
- The Redemption church competes for donations and for the Bazaar door.
- The police are weak and rarely present.

## 14. Religion and superstition

- **Redemption church**: preaching, prayer and pea soup. Donors get a Bazaar door key.
- **Father Tobias**: a priest in Deekula, sick, who says he has heard of an other side and must investigate before speaking. As a shadow he is in between.
- **The swamp church**: a church said to be in the swamp, where the Stalburg Underground's cult members may be found.
- **The Stalburg Underground**: a group that changes people with a drink and a rage, which promises the truth and a path behind the gate.
- **Welhö's mysticism** is the only spiritual thread besides the priests: the Welho's Mark, the Sacred Seal of Welhous and the Suurwelho.
- **Curses**: the blue mushroom cave is cursed; the B2 tunnel's mannequin is cursed; the rubber duck is cursed. The Spore-Blight Curse can strike people who read the book or see the Other Side (Welhö's claim).
- **Ghosts and the in-between**: the dead wait in the wasteland.

## 15. Geography

- Open Sewer sits beside an old drainage canal fed from Black Swamp. The main canal can burn skin; the side branch is only murky.
- The tenement district is cramped. The Bazaar is walled, with a gate.
- The mines are below the district. The service area is a central junction with elevators.
- The Bergmann water tunnels run under the Bazaar and reach the bank.
- The metro runs in old tunnels.
- The city gate sits at the edge of the district. The wasteland is beyond it.
- Other places are outside: Stalburg Central (permit needed), Orefell, Cellecter, Uhlu, Whiprock island (planned quarantine), Hammer Valley, Castle Rock Island (across a drawbridge), and Turnip hill.
- Italy and Poland appear only in item texts.

## 16. Tone and memorable details

The tone is bleak, sardonic and grimly comic. Dark jokes sit beside real misery. Examples, paraphrased:

- The intake interviewer admits that something about the arrangement is wrong.
- Mr. Duck's quack speech turns every line into a joke.
- Welhö says some gates should stay closed.
- Irma Kött insists her meat is not a mystery.
- Dog meat is sold as bread with animal cruelty in the Bazaar's jokes.
- The Crazy Neighbor addresses the player as his neighbour in every line.
- Tähti Kattila wonders whether falling from a high place would hurt. This is a serious and dark line.

Treat the dark lines with care.
