# Obenseuer: Overview, Story Arc and Timeline

Scope: the premise, the setting, the player's situation, the main story lines and their order as far as the game text shows, the possible endings and branches, and the in-world timeline with the dates the texts give. Covers all eight digests and the raw text files they cite. Character, place, quest, world and open-question details are in the other documents.
Status: unverified source (digested by a model from game text, not played)
Related: the other lore documents
**SPOILERS: this document tells the whole story of the game.**

---

## 1. Premise

Obenseuer is a quarantined tenement district of Stalburg, the city in the country of Stolland. Locals call it "Open Sewer", after an old drainage canal fed from Black Swamp that has turned into an open sewer. The district is a grim, cramped, poor place: tenement blocks, a Bazaar market enclave with its own gate, mines under the ground, a metro line, and a wasteland past the city gate.

The source of the trouble is a fungal disease that the texts call "fungal disease" almost everywhere. Only one source gives it a full name ("Stalburg Mushroom Virus", abbreviated SMV). It is spread or triggered by green glowing mushrooms, which are also the district's main drug. The government has responded by quarantining infected people inside the district, refusing to expand it, and stopping the issue of new passports. The Bazaar, the mines and the Redemption church all make money or hope out of the situation.

The player character is a newcomer who has been sent to Obenseuer. The default name in the variables file is Esko Virtanen. Several characters assume the player is an outsider, and some lines suggest the player was brought here for reasons they do not yet understand.

## 2. The player's situation at the start

The sources give two openings, and they do not agree (see the open questions document).

- **Intake interview** (conversation [83]). An interviewer asks personal questions, which they say are needed to find a cure for the fungal disease. The interviewer then hands the player a tenement block on a map and says it belongs to the player. The player is told that OC is the local currency, that the city is full of sick and unusual people, that green mushrooms must be avoided, and that trips outside the district need a later application. The interviewer admits, near the end, that something about the arrangement is wrong. The player's package waits at a vending machine.
- **Cutscene** (conversation [415]). A different briefing: the player is the last of six people sent ahead, under a non-disclosure agreement. On arrival the player must dig red rocks out of an excavation with a pickaxe. Success revokes a pending relocation to Obenseuer. Welhö then appears and the scene becomes a card game (see the Welho line below).

In either version the player starts with an unfinished, poorly maintained tenement, which becomes the centre of the game. The tenement is renovated with contractors (Samuel Jonasson in Deekula), rented to tenants, and can be given an upgrade path (balcony, facade, courtyard, walls). Rent and renovation are paid partly in OC and partly in RM. Renovation has a timer and an installment system; rent and fees are the main income. Tenants arrive through many dialogues, and many NPCs ask for an apartment.

The other early pressure is access. Many places need a passport: the Bazaar gate, the bank, the mining permit, the elevator to the surface. Passports are no longer issued legally, so the player must buy or forge one.

## 3. The overall story arc, as far as the sources show

The arc below follows the order the texts suggest. Where the files do not show the order, the sections say so.

### 3.1 Settling in and the tenement (early game)

The first contacts are the tenement residents. Malone Petronis, a former Devil's Angels leader and an ill, coughing ex-salesman of orange mushroom powder, claims the building was given to the player "by the rats" and then offers to advise on running it if he can keep an apartment. He sends the player to the contractor Samuel Jonasson and to a Bazaar firm for renovation. Pate Rantanen asks for turnip moonshine for a sentient turnip in his head. Mr. Duck, a hiding tenant with a rubber duck obsession, asks for live ducks and an axe. Oscar Nilsson, a father looking for a three-room flat, has been cheated out of more than 1000 RM by a fraudster. Pia and her friend Alice are introduced in the house next to Deekula C.

The Crazy Neighbor lives in one of the player's own apartments. He is a comic, paranoid, recurring figure who demands visits and teleports the player away if they trespass on his door. His thread runs for the whole game.

### 3.2 Passports and the Bazaar

The Bazaar gate needs a passport. The gatehouse official Axel Norberg refuses entry without one, can take a registration form at 100 RM, and sends the player to prison if they refuse to show their bag. Passports are the first real goal.

The passport route runs through Passmore, who lives in Tenement B behind the O-market. He says he no longer sells passports and sends the player to his former partner Eliana Passmore, a photographer in the Kolhola building who forges them. Eliana takes a photo and the passport arrives by mail a couple of days later. Passmore's price for a "real" route is two jobs: check whether the Bergmann water tunnels reach the Bazaar bank, and get the forged passport. A second route is David Lund's hand-made passports, sold for 100 RM. A third is Hank Möbelmann's Passmore route for about 100 RM, using the password [COPYRIGHT].

With a passport the player can enter the Bazaar, which is a gated district of shops, a casino, a jail, a bank, a gym, a sauna, a bookshop, the Pontikka moonshining co-op, the Murju block and a slum. The Redemption church's Fidan Tanner can also give a Bazaar door key after 500 donations, if the player has a passport and proof of residence.

### 3.3 The Bank of Bazaar heist and the casino

Passmore reveals that he and a "buddy" plan to rob the Bank of Bazaar. The route goes through the Bergmann water tunnels, with a drain cover that opens from 23:00 to 03:00. Four dynamite are needed (the text also says one is enough). Cameras inside can be disabled from the boss's room upstairs, and the vault has an alarm.

The heist is pre-empted: someone else reached the vault first. The culprit is Heikki Mailanen, who robbed the vault with the bank manager Theo Tammi's help. The manager took most of the money and left toward the central area. Heikki hides in Mentti B. He later says he kept about 20 percent and paid it to the casino owner Casper Colikke as a debt. Passmore hands the player half of the money, and the police can be told about Passmore, which makes them dismiss him.

Casper Colikke, a sick and nervous casino owner, hires the player to find Heikki. He later offers the player a security job. Heikki asks the player to get rid of Casper, by lying, poison, or a neighbour's help, and to take over the casino. The Crazy Neighbor can take the casino and hire Casper as a manager. Seamus Liepa, the mine farmer and former gang member, forces Casper to sign the casino over and leave Obenseuer. Heikki then takes the casino back and pays a reward with a large coin mascot. The two outcomes are presented as alternatives in different scripts, so the exact final state is unclear.

### 3.4 Underground: Anya, Hanss and the gate

A woman called Anya, met in the basement of Deekula B, asks the player to find Hanss. Hanss is an urban explorer who went into the mines with glow sticks and a friend, Lukas, and has not come back. Anya is threatening: she says she would have to get rid of the player if they refuse. Eliana gives the basement door password [COPYRIGHT].

In the mines there is a collapse. The player finds a pipe, a huge gate, and a collapsed cave next to a settlement built by someone unidentified. The gate's keypad matches the interview number, [COPYRIGHT], and the player's answer [COPYRIGHT] is flagged as correct. The gate is opened with a device called Osrat, which speaks about "G.A.L.E.N". Anya wants the player to take samples to Fredrik and later to Karl Hagen, the Bazaar leader, for another route to Hanss. Hanss is later said to live in a basement flat (B2), and a "mannequin monster" appears in tunnel B2.

The Osrat device is a radio-like device that detects signals from "the other side". The texts say it needs an amulet (a Black Amulet, found at the priest's flat) to get a signal, and the signal can reach a "black noise" state.

### 3.5 Red mushrooms, Fredrik and the experiment

Red mushrooms grow on a body near the gate and are reported in the news. Fredrik, a doctor and unofficial scientist in Tenement A apartment A10, wants them for an experiment. He runs a log ([COPYRIGHT]) on a patient in an operating room, who has a fungal infection and is in constant pain. The player gives the patient red extract through an IV bag, and the patient becomes aggressive and her body cannot take it. Fredrik offers 1500 RM. He then sends the extract on to Anya.

Fredrik also supplies medicine to Pia, his patient, who has had the infection for years. In the branch where the player gives the red extract to Pia, Pia dies, and Fredrik says he had half expected something like that.

### 3.6 Pia, Alice and Joel

Alice is Pia's friend and carer, and she brings Pia her medicine. Alice has disappeared. The clues point at Joel Hietala, Alice's boyfriend (per the tasks), who lives in Tenement A, and at a woman named Ines Hietala who works in the mines. The murder is pinned on several suspects in different branches. The body of a woman with a ritual-looking setup is found in a storage building; she holds a photo of Pia, so the body is probably Alice. Pia's body is found in her room with the head missing; the Crazy Neighbor says he recovered the head and made a lantern from it. The sources contradict on where Pia died (in the house, in a sauna, in a cell). The police are called to several bodies, but they act slowly.

### 3.7 Ines Hietala and the Stalburg Underground

Ines Hietala is found in shock in a mine room. She says she followed "the signs", that she silenced people, and that the Stalburg Underground group told her what to do and changed her with a drink. She says a rage took her and that she killed Alice, whom she also calls Joel's girlfriend. She says she wanted to make amends and asked the group for a ritual for Alice's shadow. She offers the player a drink to "see the truth". Drinking it gives visions of a floating head (Kasa) and a phone in a hole. Ines's branches end in her death by axe, the police, the player leaving her alone, or the player seeking the cultists through a church in the swamp.

### 3.8 The weird strand, the phone and the Other Side

After the drink, the floating head Kasa says the player "made it" from things they shoved down and that it will explode unless the phone is answered. A phone voice asks whether the mission is done. It says no one has survived the job, that the player was hired for hope, desperation and stupidity, and that the job is to collect red rocks. Welhö, a Bazaar eccentric, has been on the Other Side and warns against the voice and the red rocks. He says his expedition lost its sanity there and that research stations were left behind.

Father Tobias (a priest seen as a shadow) tells the player they should not be here, that something flows out of them, and that they are not the original source of whatever is happening. If everything falls apart, everything including the player disappears. Anya tells the player that the body found in a field nearby is the player's own body, and that the police identified it. This is the strongest hint in the sources that the player may be dead or in an in-between state. The sources do not resolve it.

### 3.9 Welho, the trials and the Sacred Seal

Welhö is a Bazaar shopkeeper who calls himself a true Welho, an alchemist and a member of the alchemists' guild. He has a bookshop with a secret lair, opened by a passcode ([COPYRIGHT], again). He sends the player to the Suurwelho, identified in conversation [405] as Erto Lagerfeld, a drunk man on the top floor of Murju B who claims to be the real Welho. The player performs trials: silence a noisy neighbour, fetch an item from a cage, bring a live pig, 24 bottles of Osmo Olut, a rubber duck, a red hat and a jacket. Erto then gives the Sacred Seal of Welhous. The seal opens the lair and the bookshop's secret stock.

The book [COPYRIGHT] is the Welho plot's key object. Welhö ordered his former apprentice Mikael Arnaldur to burn the last two copies. Mikael kept nine pages. The player can return the pages and be rewarded, or report that Mikael failed, which strips Mikael of his title.

### 3.10 Mines, Seamus and the Devil's Angels

Malone's old friend Seamus Liepa was thought dead; Malone wanted his body found. Seamus is alive in the mines, growing potatoes in an underground farm near an underground lake. He has a silver ring with engraved initials, which could prove his identity, and which a creature called Saunaklonkku keeps. Seamus says the Devil's Angels grew orange mushrooms under Malone and then green ones, and that Malone and Harold Rimas wrecked the gang. Malone is very ill. In one branch Seamus says he will get rid of Malone. In another Malone's last lines suggest he dies. The sources do not settle whether Malone is dead.

### 3.11 The tenement's tunnel and the repository

The building that the player owns is above a mine tunnel that is unstable. Toivo of OS Mining Services says the tenement is dangerous and will collapse without repairs. Ken Nakahara, a Japanese man who came for a week and was held by a fungal screening, says his team will reinforce the tunnel once the path is cleared, which he will pay 10000 RM for. Heska Pavonen guards a radioactive waste repository (a dumping tunnel where a spent fuel cask broke), and his gate code is [COPYRIGHT]. The repository route leads to "the other side" and is blocked by a collapse.

### 3.12 Side threads that may affect the main arc

- Ville Sköldgangster pays the player to sabotage the Svamppatrullen gang, with a poisoned Socca Junior bottle. The bottle is for the boss, who is in jail.
- Max Masher, a mash maker in Deekula B apartment B10, has been hurt. Pentti Penttilä, a speakeasy owner, asks the player to find him and to restart his mash supply.
- Arvo Kirkastaja of the Moonshining Co-op (Pontikka) sells membership for a 1300 RM fee after education, recommendations and an exam.
- David Lund's toll bridge in Deekula, which he will sell for a lifetime pass.
- Samuel Jonasson renovates tenements.
- The Redemption church's Fidan Tanner trades pea soup for donations.
- Amanda Korhonen in Deekula B3 and a body in the bathroom of a neighbour's flat, with a woman in bed who cannot speak.
- Krista Annina at the burial ground, who wants to drink a green mushroom dream to see "the black world".
- A hippie's lost pink dock, a Wasteland shadow, a phone line with the Stalburg Bank, and a telemarketing job for the OS magazine.

## 4. Possible endings and branches

The sources contain several end states, and not all are shown as final. In approximate order of how far they go:

1. **SMV ending** (script [84]). The player, now a subject, is examined by researchers. An experimental drug is given, both hands are amputated, part of the brain is removed, the fungal spread is not stopped, and the right eye is fully green. The body is full of mushrooms but still alive. The fungus is red. The subject is moved to the quarantine zone and the scene ends with the researchers planning to take the subject further later.
2. **Death and the in-between.** A shadow in a shed urges the player to find something, and the scene ends. The shadow priest, the shadow Mike and the other ghosts all suggest the player is among the dead or nearly so. Anya's line about the player's own body in the field is a strong hint.
3. **Collapse of the tenement.** Toivo and Ken describe a tunnel under the building that is unstable. If it is not reinforced the building falls. Tobias's line that everything, the player included, would vanish may be linked.
4. **Imprisonment.** Trespass, bag refusal and bad answers to police send the player to prison for a sentence set by a judge. The Warden can sell items to a prisoner to shorten the sentence, but this is conditional on need stats.
5. **Eviction and expulsion.** The Crazy Neighbor, Malone and some tenants can be evicted or can leave. Some tenant branches end with the Relay evict scripts.
6. **Welho's dismissal.** A player who gives up on the Welho path is mocked as a quitter. Failing Welhö's tests strips Mikael's title.
7. **Heist failure or success.** Passmore's bank plan may succeed or be beaten to the vault by Heikki. Going to the police about Passmore ends his cooperation.
8. **Ines's endings.** Kill her with an axe (she dies), hand her to the police, leave her alone (quest done), or go looking for the cult in the swamp.
9. **Red extract experiment.** If the extract is lost, Fredrik says the test cannot be completed, and the reward is lost.

Other branches are short and reversible (for example refusing a deal with Mr. Duck, or accepting Mikkel Purola's minitrain bargain). They are listed in the quest document.

## 5. Main story lines in short

- **Housing and tenements.** The player renovates a building, rents apartments to tenants, and deals with tenant crises. This is the economic engine and the reason many NPCs appear.
- **Passports and the Bazaar.** The player buys or forges papers to enter the Bazaar and then gets into its banks, casino, co-op and church.
- **The Bank and casino.** A heist is planned, pre-empted by Heikki Mailanen, and the casino passes between owners.
- **The mines and the gate.** The search for Hanss leads into the mines, to a gate and to Osrat's signal of "the other side". Red mushrooms and a radioactive repository are the physical hazards.
- **Red mushrooms, experiments and murder.** Fredrik's experiment, Pia's death, Alice's murder and Ines's confession are linked by the red extract.
- **The Other Side.** Welhö, the phone voice, the shadows and Tobias all point at a metaphysical layer. The player may already be on the wrong side of it.

## 7. The Welho and Osrat lines, briefly

The Welho storyline and the Osrat device both point at the Other Side, the same layer the phone voice and Tobias hint at. The source for this link is the Welhö dialogue, the Osrat notes, and the phone voice. The texts do not say explicitly that they are one mystery. A reader who puts the pieces together gets: a gate, a device that needs an amulet, a red rock that pulls, a voice that asks for the rocks, and a leader who lost his mind after going through. The texts never say what the Other Side is, which is the most important open question in the whole game.

## 8. Notes on reliability

- Most of the story comes from dialogue conditions and scripts. Facts from conditions are described as [COPYRIGHT], not as confirmed.
- Several quest ids (TrueWelho11, casinoCoup10, BankHunt77, aziz0, joinpontikka10, BazaarPermit44 and others) appear only in conditions.
- Two intro scenes disagree (see section 2).
- Items and notes carry much of the world's tone and some of its plot, but they are short.
- The game has no finished ending text in these files. The SMV ending is the only fully written end scene in the sources read.
